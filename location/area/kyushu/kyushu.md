# Kyushu

Kyushu region spans the same territory as the island, anchored by Fukuoka's food scene and a chain of volcanic hot spring towns. Highlights include Beppu and Yufuin's onsen culture, Aso's volcanic caldera, and Kagoshima's view of the ever-active Sakurajima.

## Prefectures

- **Fukuoka** — Fukuoka is Kyushu's gateway city, famous for Hakata-style tonkotsu ramen, open-air yatai food stalls, and Dazaifu Tenmangu, a major shrine dedicated to the god of learning.
- **Kagoshima** — Kagoshima sits in the shadow of the ever-active Sakurajima volcano and is known for kurobuta black pork, sweet potato shochu, and the sand bath hot springs of Ibusuki.
- **Kumamoto** — Kumamoto features one of Japan's most impressive castles, the volcanic caldera of Mount Aso, the beloved black bear mascot Kumamon, and the mountain hot spring village of Kurokawa Onsen.
- **Miyazaki** — Miyazaki offers the mythology-steeped Takachiho Gorge, laid-back surfing beaches, a warm subtropical climate, and some of Japan's best mangoes.
- **Nagasaki** — A historic trading port shaped by centuries of Dutch and Chinese contact, Nagasaki is home to the Peace Park, the Huis Ten Bosch theme park, champon noodles, and the atmospheric ruins of Gunkanjima island.
- **Oita** — Oita is Japan's onsen capital, led by Beppu's steaming 'hells' and the elegant ryokan town of Yufuin, with a local cuisine built around hot-spring steamed dishes.
- **Saga** — Saga is known for the fine porcelain of Arita and Imari, the ancient Yoshinogari archaeological site, and its annual international hot air balloon festival.
