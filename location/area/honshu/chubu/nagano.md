# Nagano

Set in the Japanese Alps, Nagano is known for ski resorts like Hakuba, Matsumoto Castle, the ancient temple Zenko-ji, snow monkeys bathing in hot springs at Jigokudani, and soba noodle culture.

## Cities

- **Azumino** — Known for its wasabi farms and views of the Northern Japan Alps.
- **Chikuma** — Known for the Obasute rice terraces, famous for moon-viewing.
- **Chino** — Home to the Jomon-era Venus figurine and views of the Yatsugatake mountains.
- **Iida** — Known for the Ina Valley's apple orchards and traditional puppet theater.
- **Iiyama** — Known for heavy snowfall, ski resorts, and traditional Buddhist altar craftsmanship.
- **Ina** — Set beneath the Southern Alps, known for its rose gardens and soba.
- **Komagane** — Gateway to Mount Kiso-Komagatake via a scenic ropeway.
- **Komoro** — A castle town associated with poet Toson Shimazaki and Kaikoen park.
- **Matsumoto** — Known for its striking black 'Crow Castle,' one of Japan's original feudal-era castles.
- **Nagano** — The prefectural capital, home to the ancient temple Zenko-ji and 1998 Winter Olympics venues.
- **Nakano** — Known for apple and grape orchards in northern Nagano.
- **Okaya** — Set on Lake Suwa, historically a center of Japan's silk industry.
- **Omachi** — A mountain town serving as a gateway to the Tateyama Kurobe Alpine Route.
- **Saku** — Known for the Saku General Hospital's pioneering community health movement and highland farming.
- **Shiojiri** — A railway crossroads city known for wine production and soba.
- **Suwa** — Home to Lake Suwa and the Suwa Grand Shrine, famous for its Onbashira festival.
- **Suzaka** — Known for its preserved warehouse district and nearby ski resorts.
- **Tomi** — A small city known for its rural landscapes and hot springs.
- **Ueda** — A castle town linked to samurai Sanada Yukimura, known for its historic streets.
