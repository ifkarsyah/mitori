# Shizuoka

Shizuoka offers classic views of Mount Fuji, rolling green tea plantations, and the hot springs and coastline of the Izu Peninsula. It is also Japan's top producer of wasabi.

## Cities

- **Atami** — A classic hot spring resort town on the Izu Peninsula's coast.
- **Fuji** — A paper-manufacturing city with iconic views of Mount Fuji across the Tagonoura coast.
- **Fujieda** — A major green tea distribution and auction center.
- **Fujinomiya** — Home to the head shrine of Mount Fuji worship and a gateway for climbers.
- **Fukuroi** — Home to Ecopa Stadium and traditional tako-age kite flying.
- **Gotemba** — Known for premium outlet shopping and close-up Mount Fuji views.
- **Hamamatsu** — Known as a center of musical instrument and motorcycle manufacturing, home to Yamaha and Suzuki.
- **Ito** — A hot spring resort city on the Izu Peninsula's eastern coast.
- **Iwata** — Known for its unagi (eel) farming and Enshu textile weaving heritage.
- **Izu** — Central Izu Peninsula city known for wasabi cultivation and hot springs.
- **Izunokuni** — Known for the Izu Panorama Park ropeway and hot springs.
- **Kakegawa** — Known for its reconstructed castle and green tea production.
- **Kikugawa** — A tea-growing city known for green tea auctions.
- **Kosai** — Known for its lake and eel farming near Lake Hamana.
- **Makinohara** — Home to one of Japan's largest green tea plantations.
- **Mishima** — Known for its clear spring-fed waterways sourced from Mount Fuji's snowmelt.
- **Numazu** — A fishing port known for fresh seafood and views across Suruga Bay to Mount Fuji.
- **Omaezaki** — Known for its lighthouse and windsurfing beaches at Cape Omaezaki.
- **Shimada** — Known for green tea fields and the historic Oi River crossing.
- **Shimoda** — A historic port where Commodore Perry's fleet first negotiated with Japan, now a beach resort.
- **Shizuoka** — The prefectural capital, known for green tea plantations and views of Mount Fuji.
- **Susono** — Gateway to the Fuji Safari Park and Mount Fuji's northern trails.
- **Yaizu** — One of Japan's largest tuna and bonito fishing ports.
