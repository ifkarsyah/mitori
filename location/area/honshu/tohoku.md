# Tohoku

The northeastern region of Honshu, known for rugged coastlines, rice paddies, and apple orchards. It offers deep snow, samurai towns like Kakunodate, hearty comfort food such as Sendai's gyutan and Yamagata's cherries, and lively summer festivals like Aomori's Nebuta and Sendai's Tanabata.

## Prefectures

- **Akita** — Akita is known for the lantern-balancing Kanto Festival, the hot-spring hideaway of Nyuto Onsen, and its namesake Akita dog breed.
- **Aomori** — Known for the raucous Nebuta Festival, Aomori is Japan's top apple producer and home to Hirosaki Castle's cherry blossoms.
- **Fukushima** — Fukushima's highlights include Aizu-Wakamatsu's samurai history and Tsuruga Castle, the scenic Lake Inawashiro, sweet peaches, and numerous hot spring towns tucked into its mountains.
- **Iwate** — Iwate is known for the UNESCO-listed temples of Hiraizumi, the dramatic Sanriku coastline, and Morioka's reimen cold noodles and wanko soba all-you-can-eat tradition.
- **Miyagi** — Centered on the city of Sendai, Miyagi is famous for grilled gyutan beef tongue, Matsushima Bay's pine-covered islets (one of Japan's three great scenic views), and the Zao ski resort with its frost-covered 'snow monster' trees.
- **Yamagata** — Yamagata is a fruit-growing prefecture famous for cherries, along with the Zao Onsen ski area's snow monsters, the cliffside mountain temple Yamadera, and the picturesque hot spring town of Ginzan Onsen.
