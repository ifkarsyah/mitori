# Tokyo

Japan's capital and largest city, Tokyo blends the neon energy of Shibuya and Shinjuku, the pop culture and electronics of Akihabara, and the traditional Asakusa district with Senso-ji temple. It is also a world capital for fine dining and shopping.

## Cities

- **Adachi** — Known for its riverside flower parks and traditional shopping streets.
- **Akiruno** — Known for hiking trails, caves, and the Akigawa Valley's nature.
- **Akishima** — A residential city in western Tokyo along the Tama River.
- **Arakawa** — Home to the retro Yanaka Ginza shopping street and Arakawa tram line.
- **Bunkyo** — Known for Tokyo Dome, the University of Tokyo, and quiet residential streets.
- **Chiyoda** — Home to the Imperial Palace and Tokyo Station, the political and business heart of the capital.
- **Chofu** — Home to Jindai-ji, Tokyo's second-oldest temple, and a film studio heritage.
- **Chuo** — Tokyo's historic commercial center, home to Ginza's upscale shopping and Tsukiji's outer seafood market.
- **Edogawa** — Known for the Kasai Rinkai Park and its aquarium along Tokyo Bay.
- **Fuchu** — Home to a major horse racing track and the ancient Okunitama Shrine.
- **Fussa** — Home to a US air base and a historic sake brewing tradition.
- **Hachioji** — Tokyo's largest satellite city by area, known for its university campuses and hiking trail up Mount Takao.
- **Hamura** — A small city in western Tokyo known for its water park and Tama River scenery.
- **Higashikurume** — A residential commuter city known for the Shakujii River's springs.
- **Higashimurayama** — A residential city known for the Sayama hills and green tea fields.
- **Higashiyamato** — A residential city in western Tokyo known for its green spaces.
- **Hino** — Historically tied to the Shinsengumi samurai police force of the late Edo period.
- **Inagi** — A residential city along the Tama River known for pear orchards.
- **Itabashi** — A residential ward known for its printing industry and riverside parks.
- **Katsushika** — Home to the historic Shibamata district featured in classic Japanese film.
- **Kita** — Known for the Asukayama cherry blossom park along the Sumida River.
- **Kiyose** — Known for its cherry blossom-lined streets and proximity to Saitama.
- **Kodaira** — A residential city known for its greenbelt parks and university campuses.
- **Koganei** — Known for Koganei Park and the Edo-Tokyo Open Air Architectural Museum.
- **Kokubunji** — Named for an ancient provincial temple, now a quiet residential and commuter city.
- **Komae** — One of Tokyo's smallest cities, a quiet residential area along the Tama River.
- **Koto** — A waterfront ward with Odaiba's entertainment complexes and the Kiba lumber district.
- **Kunitachi** — Known for its tree-lined university town streets near Hitotsubashi University.
- **Machida** — A major shopping hub for southwestern Tokyo, bordering Kanagawa.
- **Meguro** — Known for the Meguro River's cherry blossoms and a fashionable residential vibe.
- **Minato** — Home to Tokyo Tower, Roppongi's nightlife, and many foreign embassies.
- **Mitaka** — Home to the Ghibli Museum, dedicated to Studio Ghibli's animated films.
- **Musashimurayama** — Home to a large shopping mall and Lake Tama's reservoir park.
- **Musashino** — Home to the popular Kichijoji shopping district and Inokashira Park.
- **Nakano** — Known for Nakano Broadway, a mecca for anime and collectible shopping.
- **Nerima** — Home to numerous animation studios, sometimes called Tokyo's 'anime town.'
- **Nishitokyo** — A residential city formed from the merger of Tanashi and Hoya.
- **Ome** — A gateway to Tokyo's western mountains, known for hiking and the Tama River.
- **Ota** — Home to Haneda Airport and the traditional Kamata entertainment district.
- **Setagaya** — Tokyo's most populous ward, known for leafy residential streets and Shimokitazawa's indie culture.
- **Shibuya** — Famous for the world's busiest pedestrian scramble crossing and youth fashion culture.
- **Shinagawa** — A major rail hub linking Tokyo to the shinkansen, with a growing business district.
- **Shinjuku** — Home to Tokyo's busiest train station, the Kabukicho entertainment district, and the metropolitan government building.
- **Suginami** — A residential ward known for Koenji's counterculture and animation studios.
- **Sumida** — Home to Tokyo Skytree and traditional downtown 'shitamachi' neighborhoods.
- **Tachikawa** — A major commercial and transit hub in western Tokyo, with a large public park at the former US air base site.
- **Taito** — Home to the historic Asakusa district and Senso-ji temple.
- **Tama** — Home to Tama New Town, one of Japan's largest planned residential developments, and Sanrio Puroland.
- **Toshima** — Home to Ikebukuro, a major shopping and entertainment hub.
