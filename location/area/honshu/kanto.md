# Kanto

The most populous region of Japan, centered on Tokyo and its surrounding prefectures. Home to Yokohama's port views, hot spring resorts in Hakone, historic Nikko and Kamakura, and easy access to Mount Fuji, alongside cutting-edge shopping and pop culture.

## Prefectures

- **Chiba** — Chiba is home to Narita International Airport and the historic Narita-san temple, Tokyo Disney Resort, and the surfing beaches of the Boso Peninsula.
- **Gunma** — Gunma is a hot spring destination centered on Kusatsu Onsen and Ikaho Onsen, with Mount Haruna offering scenic hiking and views.
- **Ibaraki** — Ibaraki is best known for the Kairakuen plum garden, one of Japan's three great gardens, and Hitachi Seaside Park's spring nemophila flower fields.
- **Kanagawa** — Kanagawa features Yokohama's Chinatown and waterfront skyline, Kamakura's Great Buddha and temple-lined hiking trails, the hot spring resort town of Hakone with views of Mount Fuji, and the coastal island of Enoshima.
- **Saitama** — Just north of Tokyo, Saitama is known for Kawagoe's well-preserved 'Little Edo' old town and Chichibu's shibazakura moss phlox fields and lively night festival, alongside its role as a major commuter hub for the capital.
- **Tochigi** — Tochigi is home to Nikko's UNESCO World Heritage shrines and the Toshogu mausoleum, the powerful Kegon Falls, and Ashikaga Flower Park's famous wisteria tunnels.
- **Tokyo** — Japan's capital and largest city, Tokyo blends the neon energy of Shibuya and Shinjuku, the pop culture and electronics of Akihabara, and the traditional Asakusa district with Senso-ji temple.
