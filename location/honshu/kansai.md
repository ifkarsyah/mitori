# Kansai

Historic western Honshu centered on Kyoto and Osaka, Japan's former imperial capital and current food capital. Famous for countless temples and shrines, Nara's bowing deer, Kobe beef, and Osaka street food like takoyaki and okonomiyaki, all set against a livelier, more casual local culture.

## Prefectures

- **Hyogo** — Hyogo is known for Kobe beef and the port city's harbor views, Himeji Castle, Japan's most celebrated original castle, and the relaxing hot springs of Arima Onsen.
- **Kyoto** — Japan's former imperial capital, Kyoto is packed with thousands of temples and shrines including Kiyomizu-dera, Fushimi Inari's torii tunnels, and Kinkaku-ji's golden pavilion, plus the geisha district of Gion and refined kaiseki cuisine.
- **Mie** — Mie is home to Ise Grand Shrine, Japan's most sacred Shinto site, as well as Toba's pearl-farming heritage and the Suzuka Circuit, a major venue for Japanese motorsports.
- **Nara** — Japan's first permanent capital, Nara is home to Todai-ji's giant bronze Buddha, Nara Park's famous bowing sacred deer, and the ancient Horyu-ji temple, one of the world's oldest wooden structures.
- **Osaka** — Osaka is Japan's food capital, famous for takoyaki and okonomiyaki, the neon nightlife of Dotonbori, historic Osaka Castle, and Universal Studios Japan, all wrapped in a famously outgoing local culture.
- **Shiga** — Shiga surrounds Lake Biwa, Japan's largest lake, offering lakeside recreation and views, alongside the historic Hikone Castle and the prized Omi wagyu beef.
- **Wakayama** — Wakayama offers the mountaintop Buddhist temple town of Koyasan with its temple lodgings, the Kumano Kodo pilgrimage trails, and the beaches and hot springs of Shirahama.
