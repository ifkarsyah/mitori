# 〜のに

のに connects two facts A and B, which both hold despite the expectation that if A is the case, B should not be. "A のに B" means "A, but contrary to expectations, B too" or "B, despite the fact A."

この会社（かいしゃ）はお金（かね）があるのに、給料（きゅうりょう）は安（やす）いです。
This company is rich, but its workers' salaries are low.

家（いえ）にいるのに、電話（でんわ）に出（で）ない。
She is home but does not answer the phone.

The predicate in A is in the **short form**. When A ends with a な-adjective or noun ＋ です, it appears as な, exactly like in the explanatory んです construction ([[../../../genki-1/12-feeling-ill/grammar/んです]]):

田中（たなか）さんは親切（しんせつ）なのに、山田（やまだ）さんは田中さんがきらいです。
Ms. Tanaka is nice, but Ms. Yamada does not like her.

大（おお）きい問題（もんだい）なのに、あの人（ひと）はだれにも相談（そうだん）しません。
It is a big issue, but he does not consult with anybody.

## Notes
Because のに connects two **facts**, non-factual sentences — requests, suggestions — cannot fill the B slot:
×日本語（にほんご）が少（すこ）し難（むずか）しいのに、これを読（よ）んでください。
Compare instead: 日本語が少し難しいですが、これを読んでください。("This Japanese is a little difficult, but please read it.")

×あまりおいしそうじゃないのに、ここで食（た）べましょう。
Compare instead: あまりおいしそうじゃないけど、ここで食べましょう。("The food does not look very promising, but let's eat here.")
