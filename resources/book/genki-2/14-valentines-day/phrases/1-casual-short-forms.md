# Short forms in casual speech

Notes on the casual/informal register used throughout this lesson's dialogues.

## Yes/no questions drop か
<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けてもいい？
May I open it?
Rising intonation alone marks this as a question — no か needed.

## わあ and へえ — interjections
わあ — said when something is exciting: "wow!" (introduced back in Lesson 5).
へえ — said on hearing something amusing, hard to believe, or mildly surprising: "Oh, is that right?"

## んだ／の — the casual explanation modality
んだ is the short-form counterpart of んです ([[../../../genki-1/12-feeling-ill/grammar/1-んです]]):
こんなのがほしかったんだ。
(I've) wanted one like this.
Female speakers can use either gender-neutral んだ or the more feminine の to close an explanation.

Many casual questions end in の instead — the short-form counterpart of んですか, gender-neutral, fishing for a detailed explanation just like んですか does:
メアリーが<ruby>編<rt>あ</rt></ruby>んだの？
Did you knit it, Mary?

## Te-form as a request
<ruby>着<rt>き</rt></ruby>てみて。
Try it on.
A bare te-form functions as a request in casual speech — more politely, てください.

## なあ — exclamation
After a short-form predicate, なあ marks exclamation of admiration, frustration, or other strong feeling — mostly used talking to yourself:

<ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>の<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>はやさしいなあ。
Wow, isn't my Japanese professor nice!

いい<ruby>教科書<rt>きょうかしょ</rt></ruby>だなあ。
Whoa, this is a great textbook!

お<ruby>腹<rt>なか</rt></ruby>がすいたなあ。
Gee, am I hungry!

あの<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>はけちだなあ。
Darn, isn't that guy cheap!
