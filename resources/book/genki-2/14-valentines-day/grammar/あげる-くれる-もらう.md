# あげる／くれる／もらう

Japanese has two verbs for **giving**, chosen by the direction of the transaction relative to the speaker. Picture concentric circles: "me" at the center, "you" just outside, "others" further out.

- Thing moves **away** from the center (me→you, me→others, you→others, others→others) → あげる
- Thing moves **toward** the center (anyone→me, you→me) → くれる

With both, the **giver** is the subject (は／が); the **recipient** takes に.

私（わたし）はその女（おんな）の人（ひと）に花（はな）をあげます。
I will give the woman flowers.

その女の人は男（おとこ）の人に時計（とけい）をあげました。
The woman gave the man a watch.

両親（りょうしん）が（私に）新（あたら）しい車（くるま）をくれるかもしれません。
My parents may give me a new car.

（giver）は／が　（recipient）に　あげる／くれる　＝　(giver) gives to (recipient)

## Notes on あげる vs くれる for third parties
Between two people other than yourself, あげる is the normal choice. くれる is possible only in limited contexts where you identify with the recipient — typically an immediate family member or very close friend:
大統領（だいとうりょう）が妹（いもうと）に手紙（てがみ）をくれました。
The President gave my little sister a letter.

## もらう — the "receiving" framing
Transactions described with くれる can equally be framed as "receiving," with もらう. Now the **recipient** is the subject (は／が), and the giver takes に or から.

私は姉（あね）に／姉から古（ふる）い辞書（じしょ）をもらいました。
I received an old dictionary from my big sister.

（recipient）は／が　（giver）に／から　もらう　＝　(recipient) receives from (giver)

Compare directly with くれる — same event, particles swap:
姉が私に古い辞書をくれました。
My big sister gave me an old dictionary.

## Notes
- もらう, like くれる, implies you identify more closely with the recipient than the giver — using もらう for *yourself* as the giver is wrong: ×（あなたは）私から手紙をもらいましたか is ungrammatical in intent (you can't be "detached" from your own ego this way).
- もらう can be used for third-party transactions when you can assume the recipient's perspective:
妹は大統領に手紙をもらいました。
My little sister received a letter from the President.
