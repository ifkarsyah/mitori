# 〜てしまう

Te-form ＋ しまう has two meanings, at first glance rather different.

## Sense 1 — carrying out a plan with determination
しまう indicates you "carry out with determination" a described action, typically to a culmination point — doing something completely, finishing doing something, or having something done.

本（ほん）を読（よ）んでしまいました。
I read the book completely. / I finished reading the book.

## Sense 2 — lack of premeditation/control, often with regret
Something regrettable happens, or you do something you didn't intend to.

電車（でんしゃ）の中（なか）にかばんを忘（わす）れてしまいました。
I inadvertently left my bag on the train.

宿題（しゅくだい）を忘れたので、先生（せんせい）は怒（おこ）ってしまいました。
To my horror and sorrow, my professor got angry, because I had forgotten my homework.

Both nuances focus on the gap between what you intend and what actually happens on its own. A given てしまう sentence can be genuinely ambiguous between the two readings — which one applies depends on the speaker's assumptions when they say it. "I finished reading the book" (sense 1) can equally be read as "regrettably" if you read it despite not having planned to, or knowing it was the wrong thing to do but being unable to resist.

## Casual contractions
| 〜てしまう | contracted |
|---|---|
| 食（た）べてしまいました | 食べちゃいました |
| 食べてしまった | 食べちゃった |
| 飲（の）んでしまいました | 飲んじゃいました |
| 飲んでしまった | 飲んじゃった |

〜てしまう → 〜ちゃう；〜でしまう → 〜じゃう (depending on whether the te-form uses て or で).

宿題をなくしちゃった。
I lost my homework!

## Notes
Because しまう attaches to the (affirmative) verbal te-form, てしまう sentences can only describe something regrettable that *did* happen — there's no way to express "regrettably, x did not take place" with this construction.
