# 〜はずです

Add はずです to a **short-form** sentence to say something is "supposed to be the case."

〜はずです　＝　"It is supposed to be the case that..."

今日（きょう）は日曜日（にちようび）だから、銀行（ぎんこう）は閉（し）まっているはずです。
Banks must be closed, because today is a Sunday.

きのうメアリーさんはどこにも行かなかったはずです。
I believe that Mary did not go anywhere yesterday. (verbs in the negative can precede はずです just fine)

## What kind of belief this is
A はずです sentence states what you believe is true or likely, grounded in the surrounding circumstances or common sense — **not** firsthand confirmation. It cannot express "supposed to" in the sense of duty, responsibility, or law.

## Past tense — something expected that turned out otherwise
Turn はずです into はずでした to describe something that was supposed to happen but didn't. The clause *before* it stays present tense.

先週（せんしゅう）電話（でんわ）をもらうはずでしたが、電話がありませんでした。
I was supposed to receive a phone call last week, but I did not.

## With adjectives and nouns
| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| い-adjective | おもしろいはずです |
| な-adjective | 元気（げんき）なはずです |
| noun | 日本人（にほんじん）のはずです |

## Notes
A related, stronger negative form: はずがありません／はずがない, meaning something is **inconceivable**:
あの人（ひと）がうそをつくはずがありません。
I cannot imagine that the person would tell a lie.
