# Passive Sentences

When you're inconvenienced by something someone else has done, express your dissatisfaction with a passive sentence. Compare (a) the objective description with (b) the passive, which makes clear how you feel about it:

(a) 友（とも）だちが車（くるま）を使（つか）いました。
A friend of mine used my car.

(b) 私（わたし）は友だちに車を使われました。
I had my car used by a friend of mine (and I am mad/sad about it).

## Basic makeup
私は　友だちに　車を使われました。
（victim）は　（villain）に　（evil act, passive verb）

The "victim" is affected by an event, marked with は or が. The "villain" performs the action causing the suffering, marked with に. The "evil act" is described with the verb's passive form.

## Forming the passive
| Type | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ru-verbs | drop -る, add -られる | 食（た）べる → 食べられる |
| u-verbs | drop -u, add -areる | 行（い）く → 行かれる、話（はな）す → 話される、待（ま）つ → 待たれる、死（し）ぬ → 死なれる、読（よ）む → 読まれる、取（と）る → 取られる、泳（およ）ぐ → 泳がれる、遊（あそ）ぶ → 遊ばれる、買（か）う → 買われる |
| irregular | | くる → こられる、する → される |

Ru-verb passives and the irregular こられる look identical to the potential forms from [[../../13-looking-for-a-part-time-job/grammar/potential-verbs]] — but u-verb passives differ visibly from their potential counterparts: 読む's passive is 読まれる, while its potential is 読める.

Passive verbs conjugate as regular ru-verbs:
| | short, affirmative | short, negative | long, affirmative | long, negative |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| present | 読まれる | 読まれない | 読まれます | 読まれません |
| past | 読まれた | 読まれなかった | 読まれました | 読まれませんでした |
| te-form | 読まれて | | | |

## Unfavorable effect
Most passive sentences describe the "victim" being unfavorably affected — anger, embarrassment, sadness, hurt.

私はとなりの人（ひと）にたばこを吸（す）われました。
I was annoyed with the person sitting next to me for smoking.

たけしさんはメアリーさんによく笑（わら）われます。
Takeshi is often laughed at by Mary.

山下先生（やましたせんせい）はだれかにパスワードを盗（ぬす）まれたそうです。
I hear that Professor Yamashita had his password stolen by someone.

Compare with the *intended/favorable* focus of a てもらう sentence ([[../../16-lost-and-found/grammar/てあげる-くれる-もらう]]):
私は友だちに日記（にっき）を読まれました。（unwanted — annoyed）
I was annoyed with a friend of mine for reading my diary.

私は友だちに手紙（てがみ）を読んでもらいました。（wanted — grateful）
I had a friend of mine read the letter for me.

## Notes
- Since passive only applies to verbs, you cannot use it to express suffering from an *adjectival* situation — 私は雨（あめ）に降（ふ）られました ("I was annoyed by the fact that it rained/I was rained on") works because 降る is a verb, but there's no way to passivize 悪（わる）い (bad) to express "I was annoyed that the weather was bad." You also can't passivize an already-negated verb, so "Professor Yamashita was annoyed because students did not come to class" isn't expressible this way.
- Not every passive sentence is unfavorable — a handful come out neutral or even positive:
私はその人（ひと）にデートに誘（さそ）われました。("I was asked out by that person for a date.")
私は兄（あに）に友だちに紹介（しょうかい）されました。("I was introduced by my big brother to a friend of his.")
私は友だちにパーティーに呼（よ）ばれました。("I was invited by a friend to a party.")
その人はみんなに尊敬（そんけい）されています。("That person is looked up to by most everyone.")
- A second, rarer type — "direct passive" — has an inanimate/non-sentient subject and carries no implication of inconvenience; it's more common in writing than speech, and the agent takes によって instead of に:
ここに公園（こうえん）が作（つく）られます。("A park will be built here.")
この絵（え）はピカソによってかかれました。("The picture was drawn by Picasso.")
南（みなみ）アフリカでワールドカップが開（ひら）かれました。("The World Cup was held in South Africa.")
