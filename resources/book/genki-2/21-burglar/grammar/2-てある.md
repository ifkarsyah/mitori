# 〜てある

Te-form ＋ ある characterizes a state **brought about on purpose** by somebody who remains unnamed in the sentence.

<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いので、ヒーターがつけてあります。
The heater is on, because it is cold. (＝ The heater was turned on and has been kept that way.)

テーブルの<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>置<rt>お</rt></ruby>いてあります。
A book is on the table. (＝ The book was put on the table and it has remained there ever since.)

Use てあります when: (1) somebody — possibly yourself — performed the action on purpose earlier, describable with ておく ([[../../15-a-trip-to-nagano/grammar/3-ておく]]), and (2) the result of that action can still be observed now. てあります is present tense, describing a current state.

レストランの<ruby>予約<rt>よやく</rt></ruby>がしてあります。 is the result of レストランの予約をしておきました。
A restaurant reservation has been made. (I made a reservation in advance.)

パンが<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>ってあります。 is the result of パンを買っておきました。
Bread has been bought (and is ready). (I bought bread for future use.)

## The noun takes が (or は), not を
てある almost exclusively pairs with **transitive** verbs, and reassigns the particle on the noun — usually を under normal use — to が (or は).

## てある vs ている — transitive vs. intransitive current states
てある goes with transitive verbs; ている, describing a current state, goes with **intransitive** verbs (recall the pairs from [[../../18-johns-part-time-job/grammar/1-transitivity-pairs]]).

<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>が<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めてあります。（閉める＝transitive）
The window has been kept closed.

窓が閉まっています。（閉まる＝intransitive）
The window is closed.

Both describe the same real-world fact (the window is closed), but with a different connotation. The transitive てある sentence implies the current state is the result of a **human action** — somebody closed it and kept it that way. The intransitive ている sentence carries no such clear implication of human intervention — the window is closed, but this may or may not be because someone closed it.
