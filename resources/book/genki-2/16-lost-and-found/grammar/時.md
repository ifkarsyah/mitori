# 〜時（とき）

時 describes **when** something happens or happened.

sentence A　時、　sentence B。　＝　"When A, B."
（A = time reference, B = main event）

A always ends in a **short form**, present or past. Which tense to pick in A depends on temporal order relative to B — imagine yourself at the moment B takes place, and describe A from there.

## Present tense in A — A is current or still in the future (relative to B)
チベットに行（い）く時、ビザを取（と）ります。
I will get the visa issued when I go to Tibet.
（going to Tibet [A] happens after getting the visa [B] → present tense 行く）

The whole sequence can be shifted to the past without changing this logic — what matters is the order between A and B, not real-world tense:
チベットに行く時、ビザを取りました。
I had the visa issued when I was going to go to Tibet.
（still: A happens after B → still present tense 行く, even though 取りました is past）

More examples:
寝（ね）る時、コンタクトを取（と）ります。（you remove lenses [B], then go to bed [A]）
出（で）かける時、ドアにかぎをかけました。（you locked the door [B], then went out [A]）

A present-tense state (not an action) also gets the present tense when it holds at the time B occurs:
さびしい時、友（とも）だちに電話（でんわ）します。
I call up friends when I am lonely.

寒（さむ）い時、頭（あたま）が痛（いた）くなります。
I get a headache when it is cold.
（な-adjectives take な before 時; nouns take の before 時.）

If A is an ongoing action happening *during* which B occurs, use ている:
テレビを見（み）ている時、友だちから電話がありました。
A phone call came when I was watching TV.

## Past tense in A — A is already "in the past" (relative to B)
中国（ちゅうごく）に行った時、ウーロン茶（ちゃ）を買（か）います。
I will buy oolong tea when I go to China.（arriving in China [A] precedes buying tea [B] → past tense 行った, even though the whole thing is future）

中国に行った時、ウーロン茶を買いました。
I bought oolong tea when I went to China.

疲（つか）れた時、ゆっくりお風呂（ふろ）に入（はい）ります。
I take a long bath when I have gotten tired.

宿題（しゅくだい）を忘（わす）れた時、泣（な）いてしまいました。
I cried when I had forgotten to bring the homework.

## Notes
This grammar has more twists and quirks than a first pass can cover — but "which happens first, A or B?" is the key question at this level, not "is A actually in the past."
