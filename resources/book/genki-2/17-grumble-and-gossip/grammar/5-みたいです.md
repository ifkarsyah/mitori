# 〜みたいです

Following a **noun**, みたいです says something/somebody resembles the noun described — usually (not necessarily) in external characteristics.

<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>はカーネルおじさんみたいです。
My dad looks/acts like Colonel Sanders, the KFC founder.

あの<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>はゴリラみたいです。
That person over there is like a gorilla.

## Following a verb — "appears to be the case"
みたいです can also follow a verb's **short form**, present or past, affirmative or negative:

<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>ったみたいです。
It looks like it has rained.

あの人はお<ruby>腹<rt>なか</rt></ruby>がすいているみたいです。
It looks like that person is hungry.

あの人は<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby><ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>なかったみたいです。
It looks like that person did not sleep last night.

<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>学校<rt>がっこう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないみたいです。
It looks like the professor is not coming to school tomorrow.

verb ＋ みたいです　＝　"It looks like..."

## Notes
- Usable about yourself, when unsure of your own situation: <ruby>財布<rt>さいふ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れたみたいです。("It looks like I have left my wallet at home.")
- みたいです can technically follow adjectives too, but そうです ([[../../13-looking-for-a-part-time-job/grammar/3-そうです-looks-like]]) is far more common there.
