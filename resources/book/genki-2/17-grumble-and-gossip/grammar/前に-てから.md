# 〜前に／〜てから

Two ways to sequence events, covering opposite directions.

## 〜前に (まえに) — before A
verb A（short, present）＋ 前に　verb B　＝　"B before A."

国（くに）に帰（かえ）る前に、もう一度（いちど）東京（とうきょう）に行（い）きます。
I will go to Tokyo one more time before I go back home.

日本（にほん）に来（く）る前に、一学期（いちがっき）日本語（にほんご）を勉強（べんきょう）しました。
I studied Japanese for one semester before I came to Japan.

The verb before 前に is always in the **present tense**, regardless of the overall sentence's tense (present in the first example, past in the second).

## 〜てから — after A
verb A ＋ てから　verb B　＝　"A, and then B. / B after A."

勉強（べんきょう）してから、友（とも）だちに手紙（てがみ）を書（か）きました。
I studied and then wrote letters to my friends.

けんさんが来てから、食（た）べましょう。
Why don't we (start) eat(ing) after Ken has arrived.

## Notes
An "A てから B" sentence can also describe a state B that has held true *since* event A:
猫（ねこ）が死（し）んでから、とてもさびしいです。
I have been feeling very lonely since my cat died.
