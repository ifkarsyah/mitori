# 〜そうです (It looks like...)

Attach そうです to an い- or な-adjective **base** to say something "seemingly" has that property — a guess based on your impressions.

## Formation
| Type | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| い-adjective | drop final い | おいしい → おいしそうです |
| いい (exception) | changes to よさ | いい → よさそうです |
| な-adjective | drop な | <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>（な） → 元気そうです |

このりんごはおいしそうです。
This apple looks delicious.

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>天気<rt>てんき</rt></ruby>がよさそうです。
It looks like the weather will be fine tomorrow.

メアリーさんは元気そうでした。
It looked like Mary was fine.

## Negative adjectives — ない becomes なさ
この<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しくなさそうです。
This book does not look difficult.

ともこさんはテニスが<ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>じゃなさそうです。
It does not look like Tomoko is good at tennis.

(Alternative: put the negative on そうです itself instead — この本は難しそうじゃないです。)

## With a verb stem
そうです can also follow a verb **stem** to describe an impression/guess about an event:
このセーターは<ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>えそうです。(with 洗える, the potential form of 洗う)
It looks like this sweater is washable at home.

<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>りそうです。
It looks like it will rain.

## Qualifying a noun — adjective ＋ そうな
そう itself is a な-adjective, so use そうな directly before a noun:
<ruby>暖<rt>あたた</rt></ruby>かそうなセーターを<ruby>着<rt>き</rt></ruby>ています。
She is wearing a warm-looking sweater.

## Notes
そうです isn't limited to visual impressions — it applies whenever you lack conclusive evidence. An apple is おいしそう before tasting it; once tasted, it's simply おいしい. But for adjectives where visual evidence is already conclusive (like きれいな), そう isn't used — you just say きれいです if it looks pretty, since sight alone is already enough evidence.
