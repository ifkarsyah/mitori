# なら

"Noun A なら predicate X" says X applies specifically **to A** and isn't more generally valid. Two flavors: contrast, and limitation.

## Situation 1 — contrast
ブラジルに行（い）ったことがありますか。
Have you ever been to Brazil?

チリなら行ったことがありますが、ブラジルは行ったことがありません。
I've been to Chile, but never to Brazil.
（なら sets Chile apart, in a positive light, from the originally-asked-about Brazil.）

## Situation 2 — limitation
日本語（にほんご）がわかりますか。
Do you understand Japanese?

ひらがとなら わかります。
If it is (written) in hiragana, yes.
（brings up a smaller part — hiragana — contrasted against the larger whole, "Japanese" as a language.）

## Notes
- Particles に／で／から may optionally intervene between the noun and なら (紅茶（こうちゃ）になら／紅茶でなら); は／が／を never go with なら.
- なら reintroduces the idea of "if it's specifically X" — distinct from a general conditional; it's about carving out an exception or a positive sub-case, not a hypothetical.
