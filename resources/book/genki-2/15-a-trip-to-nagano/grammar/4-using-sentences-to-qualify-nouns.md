# Using Sentences to Qualify Nouns

In おもしろい<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>, the い-adjective おもしろい qualifies 本. A full **sentence** can qualify a noun the same way — Genki I touched on this with verb short forms ([[../../../genki-1/9-kabuki/grammar/2-qualifying-nouns]]); this lesson generalizes it further.

| Qualifier | Noun | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| <ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby><ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>った | 本 | the book that I bought yesterday |
| <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>がくれた | 本 | the book my boyfriend gave me |
| <ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>にある | 本 | the book that is on the table |
| <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>で<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>わない | 本 | the book that you can't buy in Japan |

The qualifying verb is in its **short form** — present (rows 3–4) or past (rows 1–2), affirmative (rows 1–3) or negative (row 4).

## が, not は, for the embedded subject
When the person performing the qualifying-clause action appears inside it, that subject takes **が**, never は:

これは<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>誕生日<rt>たんじょうび</rt></ruby>に<ruby>彼女<rt>かのじょ</rt></ruby>がくれた本です。（cf. これは本です。）
This is a book that my girlfriend gave me on my birthday last year.

<ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>が<ruby>村上春樹<rt>むらかみはるき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いた本をくれました。（cf. 父が本をくれました。）
My father gave me a book that Haruki Murakami wrote.

## The whole "qualifier + noun" acts as one noun phrase
<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>一番<rt>いちばん</rt></ruby><ruby>感動<rt>かんどう</rt></ruby>した<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>は「<ruby>生<rt>い</rt></ruby>きる」です。（cf. 映画は「生きる」です。）
The movie I was touched by the most is "To Live."

You can drop a "qualifier sentence ＋ noun" combination into any slot a plain noun would fill — it behaves as a single, larger noun phrase.
