# More casual-speech notes

## Particle-dropping in casual yes/no questions
今度（こんど）の休（やす）み、予定（よてい）ある？
Do you have any plans for the holiday?
ある calls for が (予定がある), but は／が／を are frequently dropped in casual speech — and か is dropped from the question too, since this is a yes/no question (see [[../../14-valentines-day/phrases/casual-short-forms]]). The question particle か is retained only in special cases, such as volitional ＋ か ("Shall we...?").

## てる／てた — dropping い from ている／ていた
思（おも）ってるんだけど。
思ってる contracts 思っている, dropping the い of the helping verb いる. These contractions also occur in long-form speech (でも／ます) but are more frequent in casual short-form speech.

言（い）ってたから。
言ってた contracts 言っていた — same い-dropping.

## だ — dropped at sentence end, except before よ／ね
有名（ゆうめい）なお寺（てら）だよ。
だ is systematically dropped at the end of a sentence, but retained when followed by よ or ね:
A：今日（きょう）、何曜日（なんようび）？
B：水曜日（すいようび）。／水曜日だよ。
Sentences ending in だよ are common in women's casual speech today, though until recently women were "supposed to" drop だ before よ.

## けど／が for background, not just contrast
けど and が don't only mean "but" (contrasting two situations) — they can also present the **background** to what you're about to say. 一緒（いっしょ）に行かない？ alone would sound abrupt, leaving Takeshi wondering what Mary means. By saying みちこさんの長野（ながの）のうちに行こうと思ってるんだけど。一緒に行かない？ — inserting けど — Mary signals she still has more to say before finishing her first sentence.
