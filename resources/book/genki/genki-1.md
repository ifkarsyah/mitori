# GENKI I: An Integrated Course in Elementary Japanese (2nd Edition)

初級日本語げんき I / Second Edition, The Japan Times, 2011.
Authors: Eri Banno, Yoko Ikeda, Yutaka Ohno, Chikako Shinagawa, Kyoko Tokashiki.

Standard beginner textbook (roughly JLPT N5 territory), split across two volumes (Genki I + Genki II, 23 lessons total). This overview covers **Volume I only** — Greetings + Lessons 1–12, elementary grammar and ~120 kanji.

## Who it's for / aim

A comprehensive four-skills course (listening, speaking, reading, writing), designed to be usable both in a classroom and for self-study — grammar explanations are given in English and are written to be readable without a teacher.

## Structure of the book

The text is organized into three parts:

### 1. 会話・文法編 (Conversation & Grammar)
The main track. Starts with あいさつ (Greetings, p.34), then Lessons 1–12. Each lesson follows a recurring set of sections:
- **会話 (Dialogue)** — a running story about foreign exchange students in Japan and their friends/family; introduces the lesson's new grammar/vocab in context.
- **単語 (Vocabulary)** — new words from the dialogue and practice sections.
- **文法 (Grammar)** — explanations for every grammar point drilled in Practice.
- **練習 (Practice)** — graded drills, ending in a "review exercise" that combines everything in the lesson.
- **CultureNote** — a short note on a related aspect of Japanese life/culture.
- **Useful Expressions** — supplementary theme vocabulary (not every lesson has one).

### 2. 読み書き編 (Reading & Writing)
A parallel, independently-numbered track (Lessons 1–12) for the writing system: Lesson 1 is hiragana, Lesson 2 is katakana, Lessons 3–12 each introduce ~15 kanji plus a short reading passage. Content/topic here does **not** match the conversation lesson of the same number — they're separate tracks studied side by side.

### 3. 巻末 (Appendix)
Japanese–English and English–Japanese indices, a map of Japan, a numbers reference, and a verb/adjective conjugation chart (pp. 352–405).

## Notes on this edition

The 2nd edition (2011) added a Culture Note to every lesson, bundled MP3 audio with the text (previously sold separately), refreshed vocabulary that had gone stale (e.g. removed "cassette tape," "LL"), and switched negative forms to the more colloquial `〜くないです/〜じゃないです` and `〜なければいけません/〜なきゃいけません` in place of the more formal originals. No grammar points were added or removed from the original.
