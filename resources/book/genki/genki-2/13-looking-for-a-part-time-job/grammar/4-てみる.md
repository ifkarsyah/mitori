# 〜てみる

Te-form ＋ みる expresses "doing something tentatively" or "trying something" — you're not sure of the outcome, but you do it to see what effect it has.

<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>がわからなかったので、<ruby>日本人<rt>にほんじん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちに<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いてみました。
I did not know the kanji, so I tried asking a Japanese friend of mine.

友だちがあの<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>のケーキはおいしいと<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>っていましたから、<ruby>今度<rt>こんど</rt></ruby><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べてみます。
My friends say that the cake at the shop is good. I will have a piece one of these days (and see if it really lives up to its reputation).

## Notes
みる comes from <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>る and conjugates as a regular ru-verb, but 〜てみる is always written in hiragana (never with the 見 kanji) — unlike the main verb 見る itself.
