# 〜やすい／〜にくい

Adjective-forming suffixes attaching to a verb **stem**; both conjugate like い-adjectives.

## やすい — "easy-to-do"
<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>う → 使いやすい
この<ruby>電子辞書<rt>でんしじしょ</rt></ruby>は使いやすいです。
This electronic dictionary is easy to use.

<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>む → 読みやすい
この<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>は読みやすかったです。
This book was easy to read.

## にくい — "hard-to-do"
<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる → 食べにくい
<ruby>骨<rt>ほね</rt></ruby>が<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>いので、<ruby>魚<rt>さかな</rt></ruby>は食べにくいです。
Fish are hard to eat, because they have many bones.

## Notes: やすい means "easy," not "cheap"
Note that やすい as a standalone word means "cheap" (安い), not "easy" — "easy" on its own is やさしい. Only the *suffix* 〜やすい carries the "easy-to-do" meaning.

## The sentence's subject can be a place, a tool, etc.
この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>はとても<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>みやすいです。
This town is quite livable.

このグラスは<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みにくいです。
This glass is hard to drink from.

## Psychological ease, not physical/statistical difficulty
やすい／にくい focus on the psychological ease or difficulty of an action — using them where the difficulty is really about a physical success rate or scarcity sounds odd. Compare:

<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えにくい。
Kanji is hard to memorize. (＝ I have kanji anxieties)

漢字を覚えるのは<ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しい。
It is hard to memorize kanji. (＝ too complicated, too many)

この<ruby>雑誌<rt>ざっし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いにくい。
It is embarrassing to buy this magazine. (＝ you are unwilling)

この雑誌を買うのは難しい。
This magazine is hard to buy. (＝ small circulation; hard to come by)
