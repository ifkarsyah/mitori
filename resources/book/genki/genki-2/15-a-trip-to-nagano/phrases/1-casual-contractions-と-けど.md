# More casual-speech notes

## Particle-dropping in casual yes/no questions
<ruby>今度<rt>こんど</rt></ruby>の<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>み、<ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby>ある？
Do you have any plans for the holiday?
ある calls for が (予定がある), but は／が／を are frequently dropped in casual speech — and か is dropped from the question too, since this is a yes/no question (see [[1-casual-short-forms]]). The question particle か is retained only in special cases, such as volitional ＋ か ("Shall we...?").

## てる／てた — dropping い from ている／ていた
<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>ってるんだけど。
思ってる contracts 思っている, dropping the い of the helping verb いる. These contractions also occur in long-form speech (でも／ます) but are more frequent in casual short-form speech.

<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>ってたから。
言ってた contracts 言っていた — same い-dropping.

## だ — dropped at sentence end, except before よ／ね
<ruby>有名<rt>ゆうめい</rt></ruby>なお<ruby>寺<rt>てら</rt></ruby>だよ。
だ is systematically dropped at the end of a sentence, but retained when followed by よ or ね:
A：<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>何曜日<rt>なんようび</rt></ruby>？
B：<ruby>水曜日<rt>すいようび</rt></ruby>。／水曜日だよ。
Sentences ending in だよ are common in women's casual speech today, though until recently women were "supposed to" drop だ before よ.

## けど／が for background, not just contrast
けど and が don't only mean "but" (contrasting two situations) — they can also present the **background** to what you're about to say. <ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に行かない？ alone would sound abrupt, leaving Takeshi wondering what Mary means. By saying みちこさんの<ruby>長野<rt>ながの</rt></ruby>のうちに行こうと思ってるんだけど。一緒に行かない？ — inserting けど — Mary signals she still has more to say before finishing her first sentence.
