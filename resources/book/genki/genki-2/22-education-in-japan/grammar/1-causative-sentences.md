# Causative Sentences

The "causative form" describes who **makes** someone do something, and who **lets** someone do something.

## Formation
| Type | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ru-verbs | drop -る, add -させる | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる → 食べさせる |
| u-verbs | drop -u, add -aせる | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く → 行かせる、<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>す → 話させる、<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>つ → 待たせる、<ruby>死<rt>し</rt></ruby>ぬ → 死なせる、<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>む → 読ませる、<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>る → 取らせる、<ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>ぐ → 泳がせる、<ruby>遊<rt>あそ</rt></ruby>ぶ → 遊ばせる、<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>う → 買わせる |
| irregular | | くる → こさせる、する → させる |

Very close to the passive derivation from [[1-passive-sentences]] — compare 話される (passive) with 話させる (causative).

## Basic structure
<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>は　<ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>に　<ruby>会話<rt>かいわ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えさせました。
（director）は　（cast）に　（action, causative verb）
The professor made the students memorize the dialogue.

The "director" decides what's allowed/to be done — marked は／が. The "cast" performs the action — usually marked に. The "action" is a causative-form verb.

## Two readings: "make" or "let"
Any causative verb is ambiguous between "make somebody do" and "let somebody do." Context and general knowledge resolve which is meant — you can't tell from the sentence alone whether it's an authoritarian parent (forcing) or a doting parent (allowing):

お<ruby>父<rt>とう</rt></ruby>さんは<ruby>子供<rt>こども</rt></ruby>に<ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>を食べさせました。
The father made/let his child eat vegetables.

お<ruby>母<rt>かあ</rt></ruby>さんは子供に<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を読ませました。
The mother made/let her child read the book.

## てあげる／てくれる／てもらう after a causative — signals "let"
When one of these helping verbs ([[1-てあげる-くれる-もらう]]) follows a causative verb, assume in almost all cases it's a "let" causative:

先生は<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>に<ruby>英語<rt>えいご</rt></ruby>を話させてくれませんでした。
The professor did not allow me to speak in English.

私は<ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>の子供に<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きなことをさせてあげるつもりです。
I think I will let my children do what they love.

## Requesting permission / volunteering — causative ＋ てください
私にこの<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>をやらせてください。
Please let me do this job.

| Pattern | Meaning |
|---|---|
| (director) は (cast) に causative verb | (director) makes/lets (cast) do... |
| (director) は (cast) に causative verb ＋ てあげる／てくれる | (director) lets (cast) do... |
| causative verb ＋ てください | please let me do... |

## Notes — when the "cast" takes を instead of に
Usually に, but を appears in two cases:
1. **Reflex actions** like crying/laughing: 私はその<ruby>子供<rt>こども</rt></ruby>を<ruby>泣<rt>な</rt></ruby>かせてしまいました。("I accidentally made the child cry.") アレンさんはおもしろい<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>を<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>ってみんなを笑わせました。("Mr. Allen made funny movies and made everyone laugh.")
2. **When the original verb didn't call for を already**: 行く and 座る don't take を, so it's free for the causative's cast: 先生は私をトイレに行かせました。("The professor made me go to the bathroom.") But 読む already claims を for its object, so を is unavailable for the cast — ×<ruby>両親<rt>りょうしん</rt></ruby>は私を本を読ませました is wrong; must be 私に.
