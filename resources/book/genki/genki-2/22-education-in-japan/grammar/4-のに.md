# 〜のに

のに connects two facts A and B, which both hold despite the expectation that if A is the case, B should not be. "A のに B" means "A, but contrary to expectations, B too" or "B, despite the fact A."

この<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>はお<ruby>金<rt>かね</rt></ruby>があるのに、<ruby>給料<rt>きゅうりょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いです。
This company is rich, but its workers' salaries are low.

<ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>にいるのに、<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ない。
She is home but does not answer the phone.

The predicate in A is in the **short form**. When A ends with a な-adjective or noun ＋ です, it appears as な, exactly like in the explanatory んです construction ([[resources/book/genki/genki-1/12-feeling-ill/grammar/1-んです]]):

<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんは<ruby>親切<rt>しんせつ</rt></ruby>なのに、<ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんは田中さんがきらいです。
Ms. Tanaka is nice, but Ms. Yamada does not like her.

<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きい<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>なのに、あの<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>はだれにも<ruby>相談<rt>そうだん</rt></ruby>しません。
It is a big issue, but he does not consult with anybody.

## Notes
Because のに connects two **facts**, non-factual sentences — requests, suggestions — cannot fill the B slot:
×<ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>が<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しいのに、これを<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでください。
Compare instead: 日本語が少し難しいですが、これを読んでください。("This Japanese is a little difficult, but please read it.")

×あまりおいしそうじゃないのに、ここで<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べましょう。
Compare instead: あまりおいしそうじゃないけど、ここで食べましょう。("The food does not look very promising, but let's eat here.")
