# Comparison between Two Items

Japanese adjectives don't change shape for comparatives (no "great/greater" alternation) — comparison is expressed by adding structure around the nouns being compared, not by changing the adjective.

AのほうがBより（property）。＝ A is more (property) than B.

<ruby>中国<rt>ちゅうごく</rt></ruby>のほうが<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>より<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きいです。
China is larger than Japan.

## Asking for a comparison
AとBとどちらのほう／どっちのほうが（property）。＝ Between A and B, which is more (property)?

バスと<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>とどっちのほうが<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いですか。
Which is cheaper, (going by) bus or (by) train?

## Notes
- In real speech, のほうが and より often appear in reverse order, which can mislead you into thinking the comparison runs the opposite way. Don't rely on word order — listen for which noun is attached to のほうが (the winner) vs. より (the baseline).
- どちら／どちらのほう and どっち／どっちのほう are interchangeable; どっち・どっちのほう are slightly more colloquial.
