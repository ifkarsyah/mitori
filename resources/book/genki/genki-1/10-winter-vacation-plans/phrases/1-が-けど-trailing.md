# trailing が／けど

が and けど ("but") are sometimes tacked onto the end of a sentence not to contrast anything, but to treat what was just said as common ground and hand the conversational turn to the listener — a politeness move that avoids asking too direct a question.

<ruby>旅行会社<rt>りょこうがいしゃ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>：<ruby>午前<rt>ごぜん</rt></ruby>と<ruby>午後<rt>ごご</rt></ruby>の<ruby>便<rt>びん</rt></ruby>がありますが……。
Travel agent: We have a morning flight and an afternoon flight, but...

Rather than directly asking どちらがいいですか ("which do you prefer?"), the agent lays out the two options and lets が trail off, inviting Mary to jump in with her preference immediately.

## Notes
This is the same が particle used elsewhere for genuine contrast ("...but"), repurposed here as a soft, open-ended sentence-ending device — very common in polite Japanese to avoid sounding pushy or overly direct.
