# Describing Two Activities

A te-form joins two or more verbs into a sequence ("I did this and then that") — it does the work of "and" between verbs. (と, from [[8-と]], only connects nouns, never verbs.) The tense of the *final* verb determines when the whole sequence takes place.

ノートを<ruby>借<rt>か</rt></ruby>りて、コピーします。
I will borrow her notebook and photocopy it.

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は、<ruby>六時<rt>ろくじ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きて、<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>しました。
Today I got up at six and studied.

<ruby>食堂<rt>しょくどう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>って、<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ご飯を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べましょう。
Let's go to the cafeteria and have lunch.

## Looser connections — manner or circumstance
The first te-form verb can also describe *how* the second action is done, or the circumstance behind it, rather than a strict sequence:

バスに<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>って、<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>に行きます。
I go to work by bus. (lit. "riding the bus, I go to work")

教科書を<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れて、すみません。
I am sorry for not bringing the textbook. (lit. "having forgotten the textbook, I'm sorry")
