# Question Sentences

Forming a question in Japanese just means adding か to the end of a statement — no word order change, no auxiliary verb like English "do/does."

## Yes/no questions
<ruby>留学生<rt>りゅうがくせい</rt></ruby>です。
(I am) an international student.

<ruby>留学生<rt>りゅうがくせい</rt></ruby>ですか。
(Are you) an international student?

## Questions with a question word
Question sentences can also contain a question word, placed where the unknown information goes (no fronting/movement like English "what is..."):

<ruby>専攻<rt>せんこう</rt></ruby>は　<ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>ですか。
What is your major?

<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>　<ruby>何時<rt>なんじ</rt></ruby>ですか。
What time is it now?

メアリーさんは　<ruby>何歳<rt>なんさい</rt></ruby>ですか。
How old are you, Mary?

<ruby>何年生<rt>なんねんせい</rt></ruby>ですか。
What year are you in college?

## Notes
- It is **not customary to write a question mark** at the end of a Japanese question — か alone does the job.
- なん／なに (what) has two pronunciations: なん before です or before a counter like じ (o'clock); なに before a particle. See [[2-identity-and-personal-info]] and [[1-time-and-numbers]] for the question words used in this lesson (<ruby>何時<rt>なんじ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>何歳<rt>なんさい</rt></ruby>、<ruby>何年生<rt>なんねんせい</rt></ruby>).
- Answering そうです／そうですか is covered as a set phrase in [[5-そうですか]].
