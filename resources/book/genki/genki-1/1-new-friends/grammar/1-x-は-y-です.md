# X は Y です

The basic Japanese sentence pattern for identifying or describing something: a noun/topic followed by は, then another noun, then です. There's no verb "to be" acting on a subject the way English works — です just marks the sentence as a statement about the topic.

## Bare です sentences
Japanese sentences routinely drop the subject entirely when it's clear from context — much more freely than English:

<ruby>十二時半<rt>じゅうにじはん</rt></ruby>です。
(It) is half past twelve. — [[1-time-and-numbers]]

<ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>です。
(I) am a student.

<ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>です。
(My major) is the Japanese language.

## Making the topic explicit with は
When it's not clear what's being talked about, mark the topic with は (pronounced わ, written は):

<ruby>専攻<rt>せんこう</rt></ruby>は　<ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>です。
(My) major is the Japanese language.

## X は Y です — identifying X as Y
The full pattern: X wa Y desu — "X is Y. As for X, it is Y."

<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は　スー・キムです。
I am Sue Kim.

<ruby>山下<rt>やました</rt></ruby>さんは　<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>です。
Mr. Yamashita is a teacher.

メアリーさんは　アメリカ<ruby>人<rt>じん</rt></ruby>です。
Mary is an American.

## Notes
- は is a **particle** — like の (this lesson's third grammar point, [[3-noun-no-noun]]), it attaches to a phrase and marks its grammatical role rather than being a word with its own meaning.
- Japanese nouns have no articles ("a"/"the") and no plural marker. <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>です alone is ambiguous between "I am a student," "they are students," etc. — resolved only by context.
- は is normally read the same as its kana value (as in はち、はい), but as the topic particle it's pronounced the same as わ — though it's still spelled は, not わ. See [[6-pronunciation-of-は]] in this chapter's phrases.
