# <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く／<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る

When you move toward where the *listener* is, English says "I'm coming." Japanese instead says <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます — from the **speaker's** viewpoint, movement away from the speaker is always 行く, regardless of where the listener stands.

<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。
(Said while walking toward the listener — still 行く, not 来る, because it's the speaker moving away from their own position.)

<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る is reserved for movement **toward** the speaker's own location.

## Notes
This is a common stumbling point for English speakers, since English "come"/"go" pivot on the listener's position while Japanese 行く／来る pivot only on the **speaker's**.
