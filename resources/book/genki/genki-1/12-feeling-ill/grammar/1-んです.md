# 〜んです

Two distinct modes of speech in Japanese: **report** (a bare description of a fact, everything covered so far) and **explanation** (framing a statement as accounting for something else — an excuse, a reaction, background context). んです marks explanation mode.

バスが<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なかったんです。
(As it happens,) the bus didn't come. — offered *as an excuse* for being late, not just a neutral fact.

Compare:
<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>テストがあります。("I have an exam tomorrow" — a simple observation)
<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>テストがあるんです。("I have an exam tomorrow" — so I can't go out tonight; implies a consequence)

んです attaches after the **short form** of a predicate — present or past, affirmative or negative — and is itself invariant (doesn't conjugate for tense/polarity). In writing, のです is more common than んです.

<ruby>成績<rt>せいせき</rt></ruby>がよくないんです。
(As a matter of fact,) my grade is not good.

## With な-adjectives and nouns — insert な
| | report | explanation |
|---|---|---|
| な-adjective | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かです | 静かなんです |
| noun | <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>です | 学生なんです |

## Inviting explanation with んですか
んです also appears in questions, especially alongside どうして (why) and どうした (what happened), inviting the listener to explain themselves:

どうして<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>と<ruby>別<rt>わか</rt></ruby>れたんですか。　→　彼、ぜんぜんお<ruby>風呂<rt>ふろ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>らないんです。
Why did you break up with your boyfriend? → He never takes a bath.

A どうしたんですか question is best answered with a んです sentence whose subject takes が rather than は (see [[6-が]]).

## Providing extra comment
とてもいい<ruby>教科書<rt>きょうかしょ</rt></ruby>ですね。　→　ええ。<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>の大学の先生が<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いたんです。
That's a great textbook. → You bet. The professors at my university wrote it (for your information).

## Notes
In casual speech, んです shortens to んだ, and んですか becomes んだ？ (with question intonation) — examined further in Genki II Lesson 15.
