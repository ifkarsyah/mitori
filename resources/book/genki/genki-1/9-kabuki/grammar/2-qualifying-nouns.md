# Qualifying Nouns with Verbs and Adjectives

Short forms of verbs can qualify a noun directly, the same way adjectives do — this is Japanese's version of a relative clause, with no relative pronoun ("who"/"which"/"that") needed.

あそこで<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでいる <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby> はみちこさんです。
The student who is reading a book over there is Michiko.

## Four kinds of qualifiers, same slot
| # | qualifier | noun | meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | おもしろい | <ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby> | a person who is interesting |
| 2 | <ruby>髪<rt>かみ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>長<rt>なが</rt></ruby>い | 人 | a person who has long hair |
| 3 | <ruby>眼鏡<rt>めがね</rt></ruby>をかけている | 人 | a person who wears glasses |
| 4 | <ruby>猫<rt>ねこ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きな | 人 | a person who likes cats |

1 is a plain adjective (Lesson 5); 2 is the X が Y construction (Lesson 7); 3 is a verb short form (Lesson 8/9); 4 is a relational な-adjective (Lesson 5) — all four slot into the same "qualifier directly before noun" position.

## More examples
あそこで<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>撮<rt>と</rt></ruby>っている 人（はだれですか。）
(Who is) the person taking pictures over there?

<ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby><ruby>運動<rt>うんどう</rt></ruby>をする 人（は<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>です。）
People who do physical exercises every day (are healthy).

たばこを<ruby>吸<rt>す</rt></ruby>わない 人（が好きです。）
(I like) people who do not smoke.

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby><ruby>結婚<rt>けっこん</rt></ruby>した <ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だち（から<ruby>手紙<rt>てがみ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ました。）
(A letter came from) a friend who got married last year.

## Notes
A qualifying phrase with a sentence-like structure of its own (as in these verb examples) is technically called a "relative clause." Unlike English, the qualifying clause always comes **before** the noun it modifies, with no relative pronoun marking the boundary.
