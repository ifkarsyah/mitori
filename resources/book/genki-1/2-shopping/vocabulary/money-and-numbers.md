## Notes (Expression Notes 3, p. 67)
- Sound changes: 300／600／800、3,000／8,000 all shift their first sound. Counters starting with h (like ひゃく) generally change shape after 3, 6, 8; counters starting with s (like せん) change shape after 3 and 8.
- Japanese uses a ten-thousands marker (まん) that Western languages don't have — 20,000 is にまん (2×10,000), not "twenty thousand." The next new unit after that is 100×10,000 (ひゃくまん), i.e. one million.
- Bigger numbers are built as sums of these place values, e.g. 234,567 = にじゅうさんまん・よんせん・ごひゃく・ろくじゅう・なな.
- This lesson's Culture Note にほんのおかね (Japanese Currency) covers the actual bills and coins in circulation: 1円, 5円, 10円, 50円, 100円, 500円 coins; 1,000円, 2,000円, 5,000円, 10,000円 bills. Prepaid cards are common for transit/shopping; personal checks are not used in Japan.
