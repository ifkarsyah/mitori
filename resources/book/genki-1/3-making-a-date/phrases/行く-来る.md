# 行く（いく）／来る（くる）

When you move toward where the *listener* is, English says "I'm coming." Japanese instead says 行（い）きます — from the **speaker's** viewpoint, movement away from the speaker is always 行く, regardless of where the listener stands.

私（わたし）は行（い）きます。
(Said while walking toward the listener — still 行く, not 来る, because it's the speaker moving away from their own position.)

来（く）る is reserved for movement **toward** the speaker's own location.

## Notes
This is a common stumbling point for English speakers, since English "come"/"go" pivot on the listener's position while Japanese 行く／来る pivot only on the **speaker's**.
