# Word Order

Japanese sentences are fairly flexible in how their pieces are arranged: a string of noun+particle sequences, followed by a verb or adjective, optionally closed with a sentence-final particle (か／ね／よ). The relative order among the noun+particle sequences is largely free — the particles, not position, carry the grammatical role.

<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は　<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>　<ruby>図書館<rt>としょかん</rt></ruby>で　<ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>を　<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>します。
topic　time　place　object　verb
I will study Japanese in the library today.

私は　よく　<ruby>七時<rt>しちじ</rt></ruby>ごろ　うちへ　<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ります。
topic　frequency　time　goal　verb
I often go back home at around seven.

## Notes
This is a typical arrangement, not a fixed rule — several other orderings of the noun-particle sequences are equally grammatical as long as the verb/adjective stays at the end.
