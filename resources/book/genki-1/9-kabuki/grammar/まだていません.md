# まだ〜ていません

English distinguishes "Zelda has lost her key" (present perfect — still relevant now) from "Zelda lost her key" (simple past — disconnected from now). Japanese's past tense form does double duty for *both* on the **affirmative** side:

私（わたし）はきのう宿題（しゅくだい）をしました。("I did the homework yesterday" — simple past, disconnected)
私はもう宿題をしました。("I have already done the homework" — present-relevant, もう "already")

...but on the **negative** side, plain past and 〜ている split the work:

私はきのう宿題をしませんでした。("I did not do the homework yesterday" — simple past)
私はまだ宿題をしていません。("I have not done the homework yet" — still true now)

まだ〜ていません　＝　"have not... yet"

This reading of ている applies to both **change verbs** and **activity verbs** (the Group 2/3 distinction from [[../../7-family-picture/grammar/ている]]):

スーさんはまだ起（お）きていません。(change) — Sue has not woken up yet.
私はまだ昼（ひる）ご飯を食（た）べていません。(activity) — I haven't eaten lunch yet.

## Notes
This is the same ている form covered in Lesson 7, extended to a fourth reading ("not yet," when negated) alongside "action in progress" and "result of a change."
