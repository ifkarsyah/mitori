# X があります／います

"There is/are X." The particle が introduces item X; place comes first, marked with に.

あそこにマクドナルドがあります。
There's a McDonald's over there.

Three ways this differs from other verbs seen so far: it takes に (not で) for place, the place phrase usually opens the sentence, and the thing being described takes が (not は).

## あります also means "have/own"
テレビがありません。
I don't have a TV.

時間（じかん）がありますか。
Do you have time?

## あります also describes events taking place
火曜日（かようび）にテストがあります。
There will be an exam on Tuesday.

When used this way, the place takes で (like a normal verb), not に:
あした京都（きょうと）でお祭（まつ）りがあります。
There will be a festival in Kyoto tomorrow.

## います — for people/animate beings
あります is for inanimate things; います is for people and other sentient beings.

あそこに留学生（りゅうがくせい）がいます。
There's an international student over there.

日本人（にほんじん）の友（とも）だちがいます。
I have a Japanese friend.

## The pattern
（placeに）　thingが／personが　あります／います　— "There is/are..."

## Notes
- います／あります are strictly for existence/location; です describes an attribute. Contrast: あそこに留学生がいます (there's an int'l student over there) vs. メアリーさんは留学生です (Mary is an int'l student) — see [[../../1-new-friends/grammar/x-は-y-です]].
- ある's colloquial negative is irregularly ないです, not the expected ×あらないです.
