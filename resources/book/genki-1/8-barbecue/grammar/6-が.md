# が (subject-marking, new information)

ロバートさんは<ruby>沖縄<rt>おきなわ</rt></ruby>に行きました。("As for Robert, he went to Okinawa") uses は because the topic "Robert" is already established in the conversation. But if the listener doesn't yet know *who* went to Okinawa, while the speaker does:

ロバートさんが沖縄に行きました。
ROBERT went to Okinawa. (with emphasis on the name, as new information)

が presents the subject in a way that "fills in the blank" left by a question word like だれ／<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>:

だれが沖縄に行きましたか。
Who went to Okinawa?
（Compare: ×だれは沖縄に行きましたか — a question-word subject is never followed by は, always by が, as established back in [[../../2-shopping/grammar/1-これ-それ-あれ-どれ]]'s どれ／どの discussion.）

Since が asks the question, the noun that *answers* it also takes が:

どのクラスがおもしろいですか。　→　日本語のクラスがおもしろいです。
Which class is interesting? → Japanese class is.

（このクラスで）だれが<ruby>眼鏡<rt>めがね</rt></ruby>をかけていますか。　→　山下先生が<ruby>眼鏡<rt>めがね</rt></ruby>をかけています。
Who wears glasses (in this class)? → Professor Yamashita does.

## Notes
This is the same underlying logic as は marking a topic ([[../../3-making-a-date/grammar/8-topic-particle-は]]) vs. が marking newly-asserted subject information — は for what's already on the table, が for what's being introduced or specifically singled out.
