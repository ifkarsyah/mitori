# 〜と思います／〜と言っていました

To quote a person's utterances or thoughts, use a clause ending in a **short-form** predicate, plus と思（おも）います ("I think that...") or と言（い）っていました ("They said..."). と is a quotation particle, doing the job of both "that" in indirect quotation and quotation marks in direct quotation.

スーさんは、あした試験（しけん）があると言っていました。
Sue said that there would be an exam tomorrow.

（私（わたし）は）たけしさんはメアリーさんが好（す）きだと思います。
I think Takeshi likes Mary.

## Notes
- と言っていました's ている form implies you were present when the statement was made ("I heard them saying..."). For a statement you weren't present for (e.g. a historical quote), plain と言いました is more appropriate.
- The tense inside the quote is preserved from the original utterance — Sue said 試験があります (present/future), so the report keeps あると, not a past-tense form.
- To say you *don't* think something is the case, Japanese more commonly negates *inside* the quoted clause than negates 思います itself: （私は）メアリーさんはたけしさんが好きじゃないと思います (lit. "I think it's not the case that Mary likes Takeshi") is more natural than 〜と思いません.
