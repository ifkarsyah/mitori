# Japanese Writing System · Greetings · Hiragana · Katakana

Front matter before Lesson 1 proper — covers pp. 24–37 (writing system overview + あいさつ) and 読み書き編 Lessons 1–2 (pp. 290–297).

## Japanese Writing System (p. 24)
Introduces the three scripts used together in Japanese text:
- **ひらがな (hiragana)** — native phonetic syllabary, used for grammatical elements (particles, verb/adjective endings) and native words without kanji.
- **カタカナ (katakana)** — phonetic syllabary for loanwords, foreign names, onomatopoeia, and emphasis.
- **漢字 (kanji)** — logographic characters borrowed from Chinese, used for the core meaning-bearing parts of words (most nouns, verb/adjective stems).

## あいさつ Greetings (p. 34)
Fixed daily-use greeting phrases (おはようございます, こんにちは, こんばんは, さようなら, etc.) taught as set expressions before any grammar.

**CultureNote:** あいさつとおじぎ — Greetings and Bowing (p. 37). How bowing accompanies/replaces verbal greetings and what different bow depths signal.

## 読み書き編 Lesson 1: ひらがな Hiragana (pp. 290–293)
The 46 base hiragana plus dakuten/handakuten (゛/゜) voiced variants, combination sounds (きゃ/きゅ/きょ etc.), long vowels, the small っ (geminate consonant), and syllabic ん. This is the first script taught since 会話・文法編 uses full furigana throughout.

## 読み書き編 Lesson 2: カタカナ Katakana (pp. 294–297)
The katakana syllabary, parallel in sound coverage to hiragana, used for loanwords/foreign names — plus the extended combinations used for sounds not native to Japanese (ファ, ティ, ウィ, etc.).

## Notes
Lessons 1 and 2 of 会話・文法編 (New Friends, Shopping) are printed with hiragana/katakana + romaji to ease the transition before students have finished the Reading & Writing track — see [[1-new-friends]].
