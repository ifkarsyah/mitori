# Te-form

A new verb conjugation — arguably the single most important one in Japanese, used (among other things) for requests, permission, prohibition, and joining two events into one sentence. The formation rules differ across ru-verbs, u-verbs, and irregular verbs, and the u-verb rule further splits by the dictionary form's final sound.

## Ru-verbs — simple: drop る, add て
<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる　→　食べて

## U-verbs — five subgroups by final sound
**Final う・つ・る → って**
<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う → 会って
<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>つ → 待って
とる → とって

**Final む・ぶ・ぬ → んで**
<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>む → 読んで
<ruby>遊<rt>あそ</rt></ruby>ぶ → 遊んで
<ruby>死<rt>し</rt></ruby>ぬ → 死んで

**Final く → いて** (important exception: 行く → 行って, not ×行いて)
<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>く → 書いて

**Final ぐ → いで**
<ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>ぐ → 泳いで

**Final す → して**
<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>す → 話して

## Irregular verbs
する → して
<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る → きて

(Compound verbs built on する／来る, like 勉強する, follow the same pattern.)

## Notes
- Te-forms and **stems** (the part before ます) are entirely different constructs for u-verbs — a common mistake is assuming the ru-verb pattern (食べて／食べます look alike) extends to u-verbs, producing wrong forms like ×会いて (from 会います) or ×読みて (from 読みます). At this stage it's easier to memorize each verb as a set: dictionary form — ます-form — て-form (e.g. 書く・書きます・書いて) than to apply the rules live.
- See the full conjugation table at the back of the textbook (p. 382) for reference.
