# Describing Two Activities

A te-form joins two or more verbs into a sequence ("I did this and then that") — it does the work of "and" between verbs. (と, from [[../../4-the-first-date/grammar/と]], only connects nouns, never verbs.) The tense of the *final* verb determines when the whole sequence takes place.

ノートを借（か）りて、コピーします。
I will borrow her notebook and photocopy it.

今日（きょう）は、六時（ろくじ）に起（お）きて、勉強（べんきょう）しました。
Today I got up at six and studied.

食堂（しょくどう）に行（い）って、昼（ひる）ご飯を食（た）べましょう。
Let's go to the cafeteria and have lunch.

## Looser connections — manner or circumstance
The first te-form verb can also describe *how* the second action is done, or the circumstance behind it, rather than a strict sequence:

バスに乗（の）って、会社（かいしゃ）に行きます。
I go to work by bus. (lit. "riding the bus, I go to work")

教科書を忘（わす）れて、すみません。
I am sorry for not bringing the textbook. (lit. "having forgotten the textbook, I'm sorry")
