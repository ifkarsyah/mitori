# noun₁ の noun₂

The second noun (noun₂) is the **main idea**; the first noun (noun₁) restricts/specifies it further. 

## As possession ("X's Y") — same order as English
たけしさんの　<ruby>電話番号<rt>でんわばんごう</rt></ruby>
Takeshi's phone number

<ruby>大学<rt>だいがく</rt></ruby>の　<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>
a college professor

## As "a Y of/in X" — reversed order from English
<ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby> の <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>
a student **of** the Japanese language

<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>の　<ruby>大学<rt>だいがく</rt></ruby>
a college **in** Japan

## The whole phrase acts like one noun
Because noun₁ の noun₂ behaves as a single noun, it can be dropped into any of the patterns from this lesson, including chained with another の or used as the X or Y of [[1-x-は-y-です]]:

たけしさんの　お<ruby>母<rt>かあ</rt></ruby>さんは　<ruby>高校<rt>こうこう</rt></ruby>の　<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>です。
Takeshi's mother is a high school teacher.

## Notes
- の is not limited to possession — treat it as "the following noun belongs to/is restricted by the preceding noun," and let context supply the exact relationship (possession, source, category, material, etc.).
- This is the pattern used throughout the lesson's self-introduction drills, e.g. アリゾナ<ruby>大学<rt>だいがく</rt></ruby>の　<ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>です ("[I am] a student of Arizona University") in the [[../phrases/7-self-introduction|self-introduction phrase]].
