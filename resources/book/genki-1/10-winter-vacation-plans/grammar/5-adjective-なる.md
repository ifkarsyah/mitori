# adjective ＋ なる

なる means "to become," indicating a change. It follows nouns and both adjective types.

| Type | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| い-adjective | drop final い, add くなる | <ruby>暖<rt>あたた</rt></ruby>かい　→　暖かくなる |
| な-adjective | add になる | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か（な）　→　静かになる |
| noun | add になる | <ruby>会社員<rt>かいしゃいん</rt></ruby>　→　会社員になる |

<ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>の<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>楽<rt>たの</rt></ruby>しくなりました。
Studying the Japanese language is fun now (though it was like torture before).

日本語の勉強が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きになりました。
I have grown fond of studying the Japanese language.

## Notes
- い-adjectives follow the same く-conjugation pattern as their negative form ([[../../5-a-trip-to-okinawa/grammar/1-adjectives]]) — a common mistake is to wrongly extend the な-adjective/noun pattern and say ×暖かいになる.
- なる sentences are ambiguous in isolation between an **absolute** change ("it became warm, so it's not cold anymore") and a **relative** one ("it became warmer, though still cold"). To make clear you mean the relative sense, combine with the comparison pattern from [[1-comparison-two-items]]:
  メアリーさんは<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>より日本語が<ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>になりました。
  Mary has become better in Japanese than before.
