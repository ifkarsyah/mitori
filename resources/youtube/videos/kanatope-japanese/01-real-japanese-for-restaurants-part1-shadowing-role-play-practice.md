### Phrase: Greeting (いらっしゃいませ)

いらっしゃいませ。
Welcome!

When the staff greets you with "Irasshaimase," just like when you nod, slightly bow your head. You don't need to bow deeply.

### Phrase: Party Size (何名様ですか)

何名様ですか?
How many in your party?

2名です。
Two people.

"Mei" is a polite way to count people: one, two, three. It's formal Japanese. Staff make it even more polite by saying "-mei sama," adding "sama." But when you say it yourself, don't add "sama" — just say "2(ni) mei," "3(san) mei."

2名ですね、かしこまりました。
Two people, got it.

### Phrase: Guiding to the Table (ご案内します)

お席へご案内します。
I'll take you to your table.

はい。
Okay.

Here, "seki" means table, and "annai suru" means the staff will guide you to your table. When the staff says "goannai shimasu," you just follow the staff.

### Phrase: Sit Anywhere You Like (お好きなお席へどうぞ)

お好きなお席へどうぞ。
Please take any seat you like.

はい。
Okay.

This is another common phrase. It means "Please sit wherever you like" — any free table is fine. Another phrase you'll hear is "Aite iru oseki e doozo," which means "any empty table is okay."

### Phrase: The Menu & Water (お冷や)

メニューでございます。
Here's the menu.

ありがとうございます。
Thank you.

ただいまお冷やをお持ちします。
I'll bring you some water in a moment.

あ、ありがとうございます。
Ah, thank you.

"Ohiya" is a polite way to say cold water. Staff often use this word with customers, and as a customer you can say "mizu" or "ohiya" — both are fine. In restaurants in Japan, this water is usually free and the staff will bring it to your table. Or they might say water is self-service — in that case, go to the water station yourself, fill a glass, and bring it back to your table.

お冷やでございます。
Here is your water.

あ、ありがとうございます。
Ah, thank you.

### Phrase: Call Me When You're Ready (ご注文がお決まりになりましたらお呼びください)

ご注文がお決まりになりましたらお呼びください。
Please call me when you're ready to order.

はい。
Okay.

This is another common phrase staff use — "when you're ready to order, please call me." It means: say "sumimasen" to call the staff over.

### Phrase: Calling the Staff Over (すみません、注文いいですか)

すみません!
Excuse me!

はい!
Yes?

注文いいですか?
Can I order?

はい、伺います。
Yes, I'll take your order.

"O-ukagai shimasu" is a polite way to say "I'll ask" / "I'll take your order." When you're ready to order, just say "Sumimasen," and the staff will come, then you can say "Chuumon ii desu ka?" meaning "Can I order?" — it's nice and polite. If the staff asks you first, "Gochuumon okimari desu ka?" (Have you decided?), you can just say "Hai, onegaishimasu" (Yes, please).

### Phrase: Ordering Food (〜一つ、あと〜)

味噌ラーメン一つと…
One miso ramen and...

醤油ラーメンも一つ。
also one shoyu ramen.

あと、餃子一つお願いします。
Also, one order of gyoza, please.

When ordering, say the food or drink name, plus the number — "one," "two," "three" — and finish with "onegaishimasu" to be polite. If the name is too hard to say, just point at the menu and say "Kore, onegaishimasu" (This one, please).

### Phrase: Drink Order (お飲み物はいかがでしょうか)

お飲み物はいかがでしょうか?
Would you like something to drink?

"Ikaga deshou ka" is the same as "Ikaga desu ka?" or "Dou desu ka?" (How about~?) — it's more polite, so staff often use it. Here it means "Would you like/need something to drink?"

じゃあ、生ビール一つお願いします。
Okay, then one draft beer, please.

かしこまりました。
Certainly.

The staff asked about drinks, so as you think "What am I gonna drink?", in Japanese we often say "jaa" while thinking. If you don't want a drink, you can say "iya, omizu de daijoobu desu" (No, just water is fine), or "omizu de ii desu," or "iya, daijoobudesu" (I'm okay) — that works too.

### Phrase: Confirming the Order (以上でよろしいですか)

ご注文は以上でよろしいですか?
Will that be all for your order?

はい、以上で。
Yes, that's all.

"Gochoomon wa ijyoo de yoroshii desuka?" means "Is that all?" You can just say "Hai, ijoode" (Yes, that's all).

### Phrase: Repeating the Order Back (お間違いないでしょうか)

かしこまりました。ご注文を確認いたします。醤油ラーメンお一つ、味噌ラーメンお一つ、餃子お一つ、お飲み物は生ビールお一つ。以上でお間違いないでしょうか?
Okay. Let me confirm your order. One shoyu ramen. One miso ramen. One order of gyoza. And one draft beer for your drink. Is everything correct?

はい、大丈夫です。
Yes, that's right.

"Ijou de omachigai nai deshoo ka?" means "There's no mistake?" / "Is this order correct?" If it's right, just say "hai" (Yes) or "Hai, daijyoobudesu" (Yes, there's no problem) — both are okay.

かしこまりました。ありがとうございます。
Okay. Thank you.

## Extracted

### Verb
- 案内する (あんないする) — to guide, show the way
- 伺う (うかがう) — humble form of "to ask" (used by staff: "I'll take your order")
- 決まる (きまる) — to be decided
- 呼ぶ (よぶ) — to call

### Noun
- 名様 (めいさま) — polite counter for people (used by staff)
- お冷や (おひや) — cold water (polite)
- 席 (せき) — seat, table
- 生ビール (なまビール) — draft beer

### Adjective
- 大丈夫 (だいじょうぶ) — fine, all right, okay

### Conjunction
- じゃあ — well then, so (used while thinking about what to order)
- あと — also, and then (used to add another item to an order)

### Phrase
- いらっしゃいませ — "Welcome!" (said by staff greeting customers)
- ご注文がお決まりになりましたらお呼びください — "Please call me when you're ready to order"
- 以上でよろしいですか? — "Will that be all?"
- お間違いないでしょうか? — "Is everything correct?"
- かしこまりました — "Certainly" / "Understood" (formal, used by staff)
